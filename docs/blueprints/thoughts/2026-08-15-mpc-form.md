# What the MPC on the homepage should be

> 2026-08-15 · **Status: in force** · At this size the machine is read by light and mass, not by detail; the figure standing on it is the instrument's output, not a person in the room.

Converged 2026-08-15, before reshaping it.

## The principle

**At this size the machine is read by light and mass, not by detail. The figure standing on it
is the instrument's output, not a person in the room.**

Measured: the chassis renders at about 325 × 169 px, a quarter of the frame width, which puts a
single pad at roughly 35 px. That is big enough for a pad to read as a key with thickness, and
far too small for any label, legend, or small button to be anything but grey mush. Every
decision below falls out of that one number.

## Decided

**Its own instrument, in the MPC family.** Not a portrait of the owner's MPC Sample. Likeness
cannot be verified at 35 px per pad, and chasing it would mean restructuring the layout — the
real Sample is portrait-format with pads low and screen high, while ours is landscape. The
landscape family the references share is already what we have, so this is detail work, not a
rebuild. No brand marks or model legends: a fake wordmark is both a legal problem and a visual
one, since at this size it renders as a smear.

**The pads glow in colour.** This is the strongest identity element in the references and the
only one that survives any size, because it is light rather than detail. The pads already light
when played; the resting state should also carry a few lit pads rather than sixteen grey ones.

> **Amended 2026-08-15, after seeing it built.** The resting colour is gone. The pads sit at
> the chassis cream, a shade off it, and the grid is drawn by the dark channels between them —
> the owner asked for a milk-tea / minimal register and picked the palest of three renders. Two
> intermediate versions are worth knowing about before anyone reverses this: mid-warm pads fell
> into the same value band as the oak desk and the machine lost its silhouette, and a dark-roast
> version read well but is not the register asked for. The pale version works because the pad
> channels were widened at the same time — same stride, thinner pad — so the lattice survives
> without the pads themselves carrying any tone. **Colour now lives entirely in the press**: a
> warm ladder of oat / latte / caramel / brown sugar, above the resting cream rather than below
> it, so playing a pad reads as light arriving rather than as the pad going dark. That leaves
> the screen as the one saturated thing on the machine, which is where the rest of this document
> already wanted it.

> **Amended 2026-08-17, after seeing it on the walnut desk.** The owner asked the body back
> to the original cool grey (`#f3f4f6`). Cream on walnut still separated, but it was not the
> machine they wanted. The pad well stays the dark mass; pads rest grey and still glow on press.

**Grey body, with the pad well as the only dark mass.** Chosen over a dark upper panel.
The consequence, accepted deliberately: the machine's entire read now rests on the sunken pad
well and the glow, so both have to be right or the object becomes a pale smudge. The desk
having moved from near-black to walnut is what lets a grey chassis still read as a silhouette.

**The figure stays, reframed.** He is not a palm-sized person standing in a real workspace,
which would fight the whole vignette; he is the anthropomorphised output of the machine, the
same family as a level meter or a waveform. That is why standing on the instrument is the right
place for him. At rest he breathes and shifts his weight; he dances only when something is
playing. Motion is what makes him legible as a visualisation rather than a figurine, so total
stillness was rejected — as was making him appear only on the first pad hit, since most visitors
never press one and he may be the most memorable thing here.

## Falls out of the principle, no further discussion needed

Keep what is mass or light: the sunken pad well and its pale surround, the speaker grille strip
along the front edge, the large control knob, the screen. Drop what is detail: rows of small
labelled buttons, top-edge button strips, LED meter columns, all silkscreen.

Express new internal features in real centimetres through `cm()`, the way the rest of the scene
already is. A pad is about 3.5 cm and a knob about 2 cm; writing them as fractions of the
chassis is how the desk ended up 15 cm tall without anyone noticing.

## The screen

Stays as it is — the existing video texture. Raised as the one open question and closed the
same day: it already works, and it already carries the machine's only saturated colour.
