# How a producer's desk is actually arranged

Reference gathered 2026-08-15 for the homepage desk vignette, against the owner's real kit:
Akai MPC Sample, Novation Launchpad Mini, Teenage Engineering EP-133 K.O. II, a Tannoy Gold 5
pair, Bowers & Wilkins PX8 S2 headphones, and a MacBook running Strudel.

## The rule that matters

A desk splits into two zones, and mixing them up is the tell that nobody actually plays here.

**Reach zone, front.** Everything touched while playing — the sampler, the grid controller, the
pocket sampler — sits in the same forward plane, close to the body. A controller pushed behind
the laptop cannot be played, so putting one there says the scene was arranged by eye rather
than by use.

**Look-at zone, back.** The laptop and the monitor speakers. Seen, not gripped.

## Monitor placement is acoustics, not taste

This is the detail a musician checks first, and the one that is most obviously wrong when it is
wrong. Nearfields go in an equilateral triangle with the listening position, tweeters at seated
ear height, toed in roughly 30° toward the listener, pulled off the back wall, and raised on
stands or pads rather than resting flat on the desk.

For a stylised scene the millimetres do not matter — the *shape* does: symmetric about the desk
centreline, flanking the laptop, visibly raised on a wedge, angled inward. That combination is
the graphic shorthand for "this person knows the triangle rule."

## Scale

Derived from the desk height already in the scene: **1 scene unit ≈ 25–26 cm**. Desk top to
floor is ~2.94 units ≈ 75 cm. A seated ear is ~25–30 cm above desk height, so a tweeter wants
to land near `y ≈ −1.0`.

## Proposed arrangement, in scene units

Desk is 17.2 (x) × 9.6 (z), top at `y = −2`. The MPC is 9 × 5 at the origin and does not move.
Positive z is toward the chair.

| Item | x | z | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| MPC Sample | 0 | 0 | the anchor, unchanged |
| Launchpad Mini | −6.2 | +1.4 | front-left, same forward plane as the pads |
| EP-133 | +5.7 | +1.7 | front-right, at hand |
| MacBook | −0.3 | −3.4 | back-centre, screen open ~105° |
| Tannoy Gold 5 L | −6.0 | −3.8 | on a wedge riser, toed in |
| Tannoy Gold 5 R | +6.0 | −3.8 | mirrored |
| PX8 headphones | +7.2 | +3.2 | on a stand, not flat |
| Mug / notebook | −7.2 | +3.2 | balances the headphone stand |

The exact coordinates are the researcher's synthesis, not measured from a photo of this exact
kit — no such photo exists. The *shape* of the arrangement is sourced.

## Worth building, in order

1. **Monitor risers and toe-in** — the acoustics tell, two wedges and a rotation value.
2. **MacBook, screen open, centred behind the MPC** — grounds the Strudel side of his work,
   which is otherwise invisible. A base plus an angled plane.
3. **Launchpad Mini in the front plane** — cheapest controller to build and the clearest
   "this kit is played together" signal.
4. Headphone stand — a stand silhouette reads as headphones; headphones flat on a desk read as
   clutter with no identity.
5. Mug or notebook — the cheapest lived-in signal per primitive.
6. One cable run — separates photographed from rendered, but only reads up close.

## Not worth building at this scale

Cable routing detail, port and jack icons, exact triangle geometry, real Strudel text on the
laptop screen (a warm rectangle with a few bright lines says "screen is on" just as well),
per-prop surface texture stories, and the football and rug from the abandoned room iteration.
